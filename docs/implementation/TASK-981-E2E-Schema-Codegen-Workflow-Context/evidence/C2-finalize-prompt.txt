RULE 1 — ATTRIBUTION. Write a statement only if a reader could point to the part of

RULE 3 — EMPTY IS CORRECT. Omitting a heading is always correct when today's encounter

Never fill a heading from history to avoid leaving it out. Never write "not mentioned",

RULE 4 — DATE EVERY BORROWED FACT. Any fact not established in today's encounter carries

RULE 5 — CONFLICT. Where the transcript and the record disagree on a material fact, the

e.g. "(record, 12-Mar-2026: 10 mg)".

RULE 6 — DEGRADED AUDIO AND TERMINOLOGY REPAIR. The transcript is speech-recognition
output from an ambient recording made in a South Indian tertiary-care OPD. The speech is
accented and often code-switched, and the engine reliably mangles medical terms — above
all the Indian brand names of medicines, e.g. "shell cal" for Shelcal, "eco sprin" for
Ecosprin, "thyro norm" for Thyronorm, "dolo six fifty" for Dolo 650. Left alone these are
dropped from the note or filed as nonsense, and real prescribing information is lost.
Repairing a mangled term is therefore expected — but only its NAME, and only through the
gate below. Those examples illustrate the pattern; they are not a list to choose from.

  6a  REPAIRABLE — names only: drug names (brand or generic), investigation and test
      names, anatomical, procedure and disease terms, standard clinical abbreviations.

  6b  FROZEN — never repaired, under any circumstances:

          score, laboratory or imaging value, vital sign, date, age, identifier;
        - laterality and site: left/right, upper/lower, the named limb, breast, kidney,
          lobe or level;
        - whether a drug was mentioned at all.
      A garbled number is flagged, never fixed. Repairing a drug's NAME never licenses
      adjusting its DOSE to match.

  6c  THE GATE — repair only when ALL FOUR hold:
        1. the transcribed token is not itself a valid medical term;
        2. it is a near-homophone of a real one — syllable count and consonant skeleton
           line up, and the difference is the kind a speech engine makes;
        3. an independent anchor supports that reading: the same term appears elsewhere
           in the transcript, or in this patient's recorded medications, diagnosis or
           department, or the indication is stated in the same breath;
        4. exactly one candidate survives 2 and 3.
      If two or more clinically different candidates stay plausible — the sound-alike
      pairs that cause real dispensing errors — do not choose. Default to no repair: an
      unrepaired term flagged for the clinician is an inconvenience, a confidently wrong
      drug name is a prescribing error.

  6d  SHOW EVERY REPAIR. Silent correction is not permitted in a legal document. Write
      the corrected term and carry the raw transcription with it, so the signing
      clinician can check it:  Shelcal 500 mg (transcribed as "shell cal five hundred").
      Where the gate is not met, record what was actually said and append
      "(as heard — verify)".

  6e  THE ANCHOR IS NOT A SOURCE. The record may confirm which real term a mangled sound
      points to. It may never supply a term the audio does not phonetically support, and
      it may never supply a number. Never convert units, never swap a generic for a brand
      or a brand for a generic, and never normalise a stated value to a usual dose.
