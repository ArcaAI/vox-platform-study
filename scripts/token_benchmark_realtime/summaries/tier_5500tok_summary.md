## Plan of Care
The immediate plan included administering Aspirin 300 mg stat, obtaining a stat 12-lead ECG, drawing blood for serial cardiac Troponin-I and metabolic panels, placing the patient on continuous cardiac telemetry monitoring, and arranging an urgent cardiology consultation for admission. A loading dose of Ticagrelor 180 mg was administered orally. Therapeutic anticoagulation with Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg every 12 hours subcutaneously was initiated. Sublingual nitroglycerin 0.4 mg was given as needed for ischemic chest pain relief, and an intravenous infusion of Metoprolol tartrate was started to achieve a target resting heart rate between 60 and 70 beats per minute. The patient was instructed to keep the NPO except for essential medications. Dual wide-bore 18-gauge intravenous access in the left upper extremity was ensured. Type and screen with crossmatch for two units of packed red blood cells was sent to the blood bank. Guidelines were initiated to hold outpatient Amlodipine temporarily and initiate guideline-directed medical therapy with an oral ACE inhibitor, Ramipril 2.5 mg daily once hemodynamically stable, together with carvedilol or metoprolol to reduce myocardial oxygen demand and prevent adverse post-infarction left ventricular remodeling. The patient was recommended a strict cardiac diet restricting sodium intake to less than 2 grams per day, eliminating trans fats, limiting saturated fats, and emphasizing Mediterranean dietary patterns rich in leafy greens, fiber, and lean proteins. The patient was enrolled in the outpatient Phase II Cardiac Rehabilitation program starting three weeks post-discharge. Patients were advised that they can generally resume driving after one week and light sedentary desk work after two weeks, provided there is no recurrent angina or exertional dyspnea. Strict emergency warning signs requiring immediate call to 911 or activation of emergency medical transport include any recurrence of retrosternal pressure, crushing chest pain, dyspnea at rest, presyncope, or palpitations lasting more than five minutes that do not respond to sublingual nitroglycerin.
