## Presenting Complaints
Patient presented with mild fatigue.

## Past History
Patient has been taking Ferrous ascorbate once daily for three months, but reported no increase in blood levels. Patient denies jaundice or changes in urine color. Patient reports having no prior episodes of blood increase. The patient and husband were informed they are carriers for beta thalassaemia based on the child's condition.

## Family History
Patient's younger sister has major thalassaemia, requiring monthly blood transfusions since age sixteen. Patient and husband tested positive for the trait when their daughter was born.

## Drug History
Ferrous ascorbate, one tablet daily (for three months).

## Hospital Admissions
Not discussed

## General Examination & Vitals
Pulse 82, BP 112/70. Mild pallor noted. No jaundice or facial bone changes observed. Spleen and liver were not palpable. Chest clear, heart normal.

## Previous Diagnosis
Beta thalassaemia trait (based on reports).

## Reports
Reports from October 12th showed Hb 10.4, MCV 62, RBC count 6.1 million, ferritin 85, which is normal, and HPLC showed HbA2 5.8%.

## Current Diagnosis
Beta thalassaemia trait.

## Plan of Care
Discontinued Ferrous ascorbate due to concern about iron accumulation. Patient advised to take folic acid 5 mg once daily. Advised husband to undergo HPLC testing before marriage. Genetic counseling is recommended if both parents are carriers, as there is a possibility of the child developing major thalassaemia. Follow-up is scheduled after receiving the husband's report or in three months.
